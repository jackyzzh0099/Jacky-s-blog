The forecast said rain and never delivered. What it gave instead was a sky the colour of brushed aluminium, two stops of flat light, and a city that had stopped posing.

Photographers call this bad weather. I have come to think of it as the most generous light there is.

## What flat light actually does

Direct sun is a spotlight with hard edges: bright highlights, deep shadows, and a dynamic range that no sensor or film stock handles without compromise. Overcast light is a giant softbox — the entire sky becomes the source — so shadows open up, colours saturate without glare, and skin stops looking like plastic.

The cost is contrast. On a grey morning nothing announces itself, so the photograph has to be carried by structure instead: line, repetition, the way a wet street doubles a facade.

- **Exposure**: meter for the mid-tone and let the sky clip. It is already white; there is nothing in it.
- **Aperture**: f/5.6–f/8 on 35mm, because flat light hides focus errors and a little depth is honest.
- **Film**: anything 400 ISO. The grain adds the texture the light refuses to provide.
- **Framing**: get closer than feels polite. Flat light rewards subjects that fill the frame.

## A contact sheet is a better teacher than a tutorial

Twelve frames of the same corner, all slightly wrong, teaches more than a hundred keepers. You see the moment the composition clicked, and — more usefully — the nine frames where you were almost there and stopped.

> The negative does not care about your intentions. It records what was in front of the lens, at the moment the shutter opened, and nothing else.

That is the part I keep relearning in engineering too: the artefact is the argument. Not the design document, not the plan, not the intent — the thing that actually runs, or the frame that actually exists.

## The technical note, briefly

| Dimension | Choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body | 35mm rangefinder, mechanical | No battery anxiety, no menu |
| Lens | 35mm f/2 | One focal length forces walking |
| Film | HP5+ @ 800 | Grain, latitude, cheap enough to waste |
| Developer | HC-110 dilution B | Forgiving, long shelf life |
| Scan | 3200 dpi, flat | Keep the highlights, decide later |

Digital would be faster and, on this particular morning, would have produced the same pictures. I still shot film because the constraint is the point: thirty-six frames means you have to decide what the photograph is before you take it.

## Why this belongs on a technical blog

Because the same discipline shows up in both: reduce the variables you cannot control, choose the ones you can, and then look at the result honestly. A test suite and a contact sheet are the same instrument pointed at different subjects.

The rain never came. I walked eleven kilometres and came home with nine frames I would show someone. That is a good morning.
